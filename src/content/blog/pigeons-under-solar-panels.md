Phoenix has one of the highest rates of residential solar adoption in the country, and pigeons have figured out what that means for them: the few inches of shaded, weatherproof space under a panel array is close to ideal nesting habitat, and it's largely out of reach of predators and homeowners alike.

## How to tell you have a problem

- Cooing or rustling sounds coming from under the panels, especially early morning
- Droppings accumulating on the roof below the array or on the ground beneath it
- Feathers or nesting material visible at the panel edges
- Birds consistently landing on or near the same section of the array

## Why this isn't a simple cleanup job

Droppings left under solar panels aren't just a mess — they can corrode roofing material over time and, in some cases, affect panel performance if debris builds up around the array's edges. Climbing onto a roof to deal with it yourself also raises both safety and liability questions, and disturbing an active nest incorrectly can be a legal problem depending on the species and time of year.

## What proper exclusion looks like

The fix that actually holds up is installation of an exclusion barrier — mesh or fencing designed to seal the gap under the panel array without affecting airflow or panel performance — combined with removal of any existing nesting material once birds are confirmed to be gone. This is different from scare devices or spikes, which tend to just relocate the problem to the neighbor's roof.
