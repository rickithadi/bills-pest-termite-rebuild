Roof rats earn their name honestly — unlike the rats most people picture, they're excellent climbers, and in Phoenix they travel along block walls, citrus trees, palm fronds and power lines to reach a roofline long before they ever touch the ground.

## Signs of roof rat activity

- Scratching or running sounds in the attic, especially around dusk
- Gnaw marks on irrigation lines, wiring, or the edges of roof vents
- Droppings along attic joists or in garage corners
- Grease marks or smudging along the top of block walls — a sign of a regular travel route
- Fruit or nuts disappearing from trees before they fully ripen

## How they get in

Roof rats need surprisingly little room — a gap the width of a quarter is enough. The usual entry points in Phoenix homes are gaps where roof tile meets the fascia, unscreened attic vents, gaps around utility penetrations, and loose or missing roof tiles. Overhanging tree branches and vines touching the roofline essentially hand them a bridge straight to an entry point.

## What reduces the problem long-term

Trapping addresses the rats that are already inside, but it doesn't stop new activity if the property conditions that invited them in the first place are still there. A thorough evaluation looks at:

1. Where the activity is concentrated and how rats are likely reaching the roofline
2. Which accessible entry points exist and whether exclusion work is appropriate
3. What ongoing monitoring makes sense for the property

Trimming back branches that touch the roof and keeping citrus fruit picked up reduces the food and access that draws roof rats in — simple steps that make a real difference between visits.
