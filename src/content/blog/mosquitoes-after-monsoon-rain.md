Every monsoon season, the calls follow the same pattern: a week or two after a big storm rolls through the Valley, homeowners start noticing mosquitoes where there weren't any before. The storm itself isn't the problem — what it leaves behind is.

## Why monsoon rain changes the picture

Arizona's desert climate usually keeps mosquito populations in check simply because there's nowhere for them to breed. Monsoon rain changes that overnight. Temporary standing water collects in gutters, drainage areas, saucers under potted plants, tarps, old tires, and low spots in landscaping. A mosquito only needs about a week of standing water to complete its breeding cycle, and Phoenix's heat can actually speed that cycle up.

## Where to look on your own property

Before calling anyone, walk your property after the next storm and check:

- Gutters and downspout areas that don't drain fully
- Plant saucers, buckets, and anything that holds a few inches of water
- Low spots in the yard or along block walls where water pools
- Pool covers or decorative water features that aren't actively circulating

Dumping standing water within a few days — before it has a chance to become a breeding site — is the single most effective thing a homeowner can do between visits.
